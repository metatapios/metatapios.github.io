Data & Code

Tools, codes and data behind my research. Most of my work relies on relativistic MHD simulations run on national supercomputing facilities (CINECA), plus a set of in-house analysis and post-processing tools.

Interactive tools

Relativistic jet propagation applet

A browser-based semi-analytic model of a relativistic jet drilling through a stellar envelope. It follows the jet head while the engine is active, the catch-up of the jet tail once the engine switches off, and the post-choke evolution – telling you whether the jet breaks out or is choked.

  • Density profile: analytic power law, toy polytropic star, or your own profile (CSV: r [cm], rho [g cm-3]);
  • Parameters: jet luminosity, opening angle, injection radius, engine lifetime, adiabatic index and model coefficients;
  • Adaptive RK4 integrator; interactive plots of head radius, energy, velocity, the dimensionless parameter L̃ and opening angle;
  • Results downloadable as CSV.

Launch the applet Runs entirely in your browser.

Numerical codes

PLUTO / gPLUTO

Finite-volume code for (relativistic) magnetohydrodynamics (Mignone et al. 2007) and its GPU-accelerated version. I use it for 2D/3D relativistic MHD simulations of jets in neutron star merger ejecta, massive stars and microquasars.

My contributions (in development):

  • GPU port of the Lagrangian cosmic-ray particle module to non-Cartesian, non-uniform grids (with A. Mignone);
  • a new cosmic-ray fluid module dynamically coupled to the gas, for diffusive shock acceleration and non-thermal emission.

PLUTO website

AREPO

Moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics code (Springel 2010). During my PhD I implemented obliquity-dependent cosmic-ray acceleration at shocks in its cosmic-ray module and used it to model supernova remnants and their TeV gamma-ray emission (Pais et al. 2018).

AREPO website

Analysis & post-processing

In-house tools (Python and C) developed for my papers:

  • shock detection and tracking of the jet–cocoon shock up to the photosphere, with a relativistic breakout condition;
  • bolometric shock-breakout light curves from a tomographic reconstruction over equal-arrival-time surfaces, including relativistic Doppler effects and light-travel time;
  • synthetic GRB afterglow light curves for structured jets, compared with the radio data of GW170817;
  • energy–velocity distributions of jet-driven outflows in stellar envelopes;
  • synthetic TeV gamma-ray maps of supernova remnants, mimicking the PSF and counting noise of Cherenkov telescopes.

Data

Simulation outputs (post-merger jet runs, choked jets in stellar envelopes, supernova remnants) are large and live on HPC storage. If you are interested in the data or scripts behind a specific paper or figure, write me.
